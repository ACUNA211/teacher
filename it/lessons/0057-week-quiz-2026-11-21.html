How to take it

No scrolling back to the lessons until you've answered. A wrong answer followed by the explanation sticks better than a right answer you looked up. Each explanation links to the lesson that taught it, so you know exactly what to reread.

A laptop's current M.2 SSD shows BusType SATA in Get-PhysicalDisk. To speed it up, you install a new M-key NVMe drive in the same slot, and now the firmware lists no drive at all. What's the most likely cause?

An M.2 slot can speak SATA, NVMe or both, and the old drive's BusType SATA was the warning sign. An NVMe drive in a SATA-only slot isn't detected at all; it doesn't fall back to SATA speed. A wrong length would stop you at the screw post, AHCI is the SATA protocol, and an M.2 NVMe drive uses up to four lanes, not sixteen. Check the manual first. See lesson 0051.

During a review you find the team manages the core switch through a text console on TCP port 23. Why flag it, and what should replace it?

Port 23 is Telnet: a remote command line with everything, the admin password included, in the clear. SSH on port 22 does the same job and encrypts the whole session. RDP (3389) is a full Windows desktop, 21 is FTP's command port, and 443 is HTTPS. See lesson 0003, 54 days ago.

A new file server will hold four 4 TB drives. The owner insists the data must survive any two drives failing at the same time, even if that costs space. Which RAID level, and how much usable space?

RAID 6 keeps double parity: (4 − 2) × 4 TB = 8 TB, and any two drives can fail. RAID 10 also gives 8 TB, but it survives two failures only if they're in different mirror pairs; lose both drives of one pair and the array is gone. RAID 5 survives one, RAID 0 none. See lesson 0052.

A technician drops an older AMD desktop processor during an upgrade. Under a flashlight, several tiny pins on the bottom of the processor itself are bent. Which socket style does this CPU use?

PGA (pin grid array) puts the pins on the CPU and holes in the socket, as on older AMD desktop sockets like AM4. With LGA (Intel desktops, AMD AM5) the CPU has flat pads and the pins are in the board's socket, so that's where you'd find the damage. A BGA chip is soldered down and never handled loose. See lesson 0056.

Staff in the break room say their Wi-Fi drops every time someone runs the microwave. netsh wlan show interfaces shows their laptops on channel 6. What's the best fix?

Channel 6 means the 2.4 GHz band, which it shares with microwave ovens, Bluetooth and cordless gear. A microwave leaks across that whole band, so channel 11 is no escape, and 802.11g is still 2.4 GHz. The 5 GHz band isn't affected (enable it on the access point, or prefer it on the laptops). A lease has nothing to do with radio noise. See lesson 0006, 51 days ago.

A user's new graphics card benchmarks well below reviews. The PowerShell check shows MaxLinkWidth 16 but CurrentLinkWidth 4. The card sits in the board's lower full-length slot. What's most likely?

Size isn't speed. Lower slots are often physically x16 but electrically x4, written "x16 (x4 mode)" in the manual, or they share lanes with an M.2 drive. The card can use 16 lanes but has only 4. Move it to the top x16 slot. A driver doesn't change lane count, a PCIe 3.0 card would lower the generation rather than the lanes, and a missing CPU plug means no start at all. See lesson 0055.

A company starts using a marketing email service. The admin publishes a second TXT record starting v=spf1 just for it. Soon, mail from every service, not just marketing, fails SPF. What should they do?

A domain may have only one SPF record. Two of them make SPF fail for everyone, not just the new sender. Delete the second one and add the service to the existing record (usually one more include:). DKIM is a separate check, MX only handles incoming mail, and relaxing DMARC hides the symptom without fixing SPF. See lesson 0013, 44 days ago.

A desktop with a recent AMD processor fails the Windows 11 upgrade check. Get-Tpm shows TpmPresent: False, and the board's TPM header is empty. What do you try first?

Recent CPUs have a firmware TPM 2.0 built in: AMD calls it fTPM, Intel calls it PTT. It's often off by default, so Windows sees no TPM. Turn it on in UEFI setup and Windows 11 accepts it like a chip. The header is only for a discrete module, and you can't clear a TPM Windows can't see. See lesson 0054.

A server's four-drive RAID 5 array reports one failed drive. The server is still running and users notice nothing. What's the right next step?

The array is degraded: still working, with no protection left. A second failure before the rebuild finishes loses everything, so replace the drive promptly and let parity recreate its data (a hot spare would have started this automatically). Nothing is lost yet, so no restore is needed, and RAID 0 would remove protection entirely. See lesson 0052.

You used a tone generator on a new desk's wall jack, and the probe beeps loudly on one cable at the patch panel. You patch that cable into the switch, but the desk PC still shows no link light. Which tool comes next?

A toner finds a wire; it doesn't prove it's good. It can beep happily on a run with a broken or miswired pair. A cable tester checks every pin end to end. A crimper builds cables, a Wi-Fi analyzer is for radio, and a tap copies traffic on a link that already works. See lesson 0019, 38 days ago.

A client wants the smallest standard motherboard for a kiosk PC that needs a graphics card and a 10 Gb network card. A colleague suggests Mini-ITX. What's the problem?

Two cards need two expansion slots, and Mini-ITX usually has just one (often x16, so a graphics card alone would be fine). Micro-ATX offers up to four in a board still smaller than ATX: an x16 for graphics and an x4 or x8 for the fast network card. See lesson 0053 and lesson 0055.

A warehouse's older 802.11g label printers can't even see the new wireless network, though every laptop connects fine. What's the most likely cause?

802.11g is a 2.4 GHz-only standard, and backward compatibility works only within a band, so a 2.4 GHz radio never hears a 5 GHz-only network. Turn 2.4 GHz back on at the access points and the printers join at g speeds. A full DHCP scope would let them see and join the network but get no address, and channel 11 is a normal 2.4 GHz channel. See lesson 0007, 50 days ago.

After moving a PC into a new case, it starts and runs normally from the case's power button, but the case's power LED stays dark. What's the most likely cause?

On the front panel header, the switch plugs have no polarity, but LEDs do (+/−). Reversed, the LED simply stays dark. Flip the plug and match the + to the board manual's pin map. A loose 24-pin would stop the PC itself, and the fan and TPM headers don't touch the case LEDs. See lesson 0054.

A user sets up their mailbox on a new phone, but it shows none of the messages they've read on their laptop over the past month. The laptop's mail app is set to POP3. What's going on?

POP3 (port 110) is "post office": it takes the mail home to one device and typically removes it from the server, so the phone never sees it. IMAP (143) keeps mail and folders on the server and syncs every device: switch the laptop to IMAP. SMTP (25) only sends, and MX records route a domain's incoming mail, not a phone's. See lesson 0004, 53 days ago.

A board's CPU support list shows a new processor as supported "from BIOS 2.10". The board runs BIOS 1.40. The technician installs the new CPU, and the PC won't POST. What's the fix?

Same socket isn't always enough: the old firmware doesn't know the new CPU. Put the old CPU back, update the BIOS/UEFI to 2.10 or later, then install the new one. Clearing CMOS only resets settings on the same old firmware. (Some boards can flash a BIOS from USB with no CPU fitted; the manual says if yours can.) See lesson 0056.

Explain it in your own words

Say or write the answer before you open each one. If you can explain it to a coworker, you know it.

Four drives in RAID 6 and four drives in RAID 10 both give half the space. Explain why RAID 6 survives any two failures but RAID 10 only some pairs of failures. (lesson 0052)

RAID 6 spreads two sets of parity across all drives, so any two missing drives can be recalculated from the rest. RAID 10 is two mirror pairs striped together: each pair can lose one drive and still have a copy, but if both drives of the same pair fail, that half of the stripe is gone, and with it the whole array. And neither one is a backup.

A coworker says "it's an x16 slot, so the card gets x16." Explain why that's not always true, and what rule decides which card fits which slot. (lesson 0055)

The physical size says what fits; the wiring says how many lanes it gets. A slot can be physically x16 but electrically x4 ("x16 (x4 mode)"), and on many boards filling an M.2 socket or another slot takes lanes away. Fit rule: a card works in a slot its own size or bigger (an x1 card in an x16 slot uses one lane); a bigger card doesn't fit a smaller slot.

A user asks for a faster CPU in their thin laptop, and another user wants to drop a spare CPU into their desktop. Explain LGA, PGA and BGA, and what you'd tell each user. (lesson 0056)

LGA: pins in the socket, flat pads on the CPU. PGA: pins on the CPU. BGA: no socket, the CPU is soldered to the board. The laptop almost certainly uses BGA, so its CPU can't be swapped: offer more RAM or a faster SSD if those aren't soldered too, or a new device. For the desktop: same brand, same socket, and the exact model on the board's CPU support list, which may mean a BIOS update first.

Explain the "old and plain, new and encrypted" pattern in the remote-access and web ports, with the port numbers. (lesson 0003, 54 days ago)

Two pairs do the same job, one in the clear and one encrypted: HTTP 80 vs. HTTPS 443 for web pages, and Telnet 23 vs. SSH 22 for a remote command line. FTP (20/21) is plain too. RDP on 3389, the full Windows desktop, is encrypted. Anything plain sends passwords readable by anyone on the path, so you always choose the encrypted one.

In one sentence each: what do SPF, DKIM and DMARC check, and where in DNS does each live? (lesson 0013, 44 days ago)

SPF: is the sending server's IP allowed to send for this domain? One TXT record at the domain. DKIM: was the message signed by the domain and left unchanged? A public key at selector._domainkey.domain. DMARC: what should the receiver do when they fail, and who gets the reports? A TXT record at _dmarc.domain, with p=none, quarantine or reject.
