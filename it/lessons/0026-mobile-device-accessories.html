Pens, headsets and docks: what plugs into a mobile device

About 10 minutes. Yesterday was how a phone or laptop connects; today is what you connect: styluses, audio, cameras, docks and touch pads.[1] The win: telling a docking station from a port replicator, and knowing why a Bluetooth headset sounds worse the moment a call starts.

Why this matters at work

A laptop on a dock usually gets its wired network from a network adapter inside the dock, not from the laptop itself. That adapter has its own MAC address (lesson 0021). So the "same" laptop can look like a different device to a DHCP reservation or a network access rule depending on whether it's docked. When a docked laptop gets the wrong address or no network, check which adapter it's really using.[5]

Input: styluses, trackpads and drawing pads

AccessoryWhat to know
Passive (capacitive) stylusNo battery, no electronics: it acts like a fingertip on the touchscreen. Cheap, works on any touch screen, but no pressure sensitivity.[3]
Active stylusHas electronics (and usually a battery or charge). The screen's digitizer reads it, which gives pressure sensitivity and palm rejection. Works only with devices and apps that support it.[3]
Trackpad / touchpadAn external pointing device, often Bluetooth, for when the built-in one is too small or the device has none (a tablet).[1]
Drawing padA flat tablet you draw on with a pen while the picture appears on the screen; for signatures, design and note-taking.[1]

Audio and video: headsets, speakers, webcams

Headsets and speakers connect by cable (3.5 mm, USB, USB-C) or Bluetooth; an external webcam usually by USB.[1] One Bluetooth quirk causes a lot of tickets: a headset plays music in high-quality stereo, but the moment its microphone turns on (a call starts), it switches to the Hands-Free Profile, which carries only low-quality mono audio both ways.[4] "Sounds fine on YouTube, terrible in meetings" is usually that, not a broken headset.

Common workaround: keep the headset as the speaker and pick the laptop's built-in mic (or a USB headset) as the microphone in the meeting app.[4] External webcams and mics follow the same troubleshooting order as built-in ones (lesson 0024).

Docking station vs. port replicator

Both turn one connection into a desk full of ports: monitors, keyboard, mouse, wired network. The terms are often used interchangeably, and USB-C/Thunderbolt docks have blurred the line, but the classic exam difference is:[2]

Port replicatorDocking station
DoesCopies the laptop's ports to a box on the deskCopies ports and adds more (extra ports, sometimes expansion slots or drives)
PowerTraditionally doesn't charge the laptopCharges the laptop through the same connection
ConnectorOften generic USBOften a vendor-specific (proprietary) connector
SizeSmaller, less expandableBigger
Memory hook

Passive pen = a fingertip; active pen = pressure and palm rejection. Mic on = Hands-Free Profile = mono, lower quality. Port replicator = replicates; docking station = replicates, adds, and charges.

Try it (3 minutes)

On a laptop that uses a dock, run this in PowerShell once undocked and once docked:[5]

Get-NetAdapter | Format-Table Name, InterfaceDescription, Status, MacAddress
  • Docked, a new adapter usually appears, often described as a USB Ethernet or GbE controller. That's the dock's network card.
  • Compare its MacAddress with the laptop's own Ethernet or Wi-Fi adapter: different. That's the address the network sees while docked.

Practice

Answer from memory before scrolling back up.

A designer's new stylus draws on their tablet, but every line is the same thickness no matter how hard they press, and the pen has no battery or charging port. What's the explanation?

No battery and no electronics means a passive (capacitive) stylus: the screen sees it like a fingertip, so there's no pressure data to vary line thickness. Pressure sensitivity needs an active stylus read by the device's digitizer.

A user's Bluetooth headset sounds great for music, but colleagues say they sound muffled and the audio turns flat and mono as soon as a meeting starts. What's most likely?

When a Bluetooth headset's microphone is in use, it switches to the Hands-Free Profile, which carries only low-quality mono audio. That's expected behavior. A common fix is to use the laptop's own mic (or a USB headset) for calls while keeping the headset as the speaker.

A user wants one cable from their laptop to a desk box that drives two monitors, adds extra USB ports and wired network, and charges the laptop. Which accessory fits?

Adding ports beyond the laptop's own and charging through the same connection is the classic docking-station description. A port replicator traditionally only copies the existing ports and doesn't charge. Drawing pads and trackpads are input devices.

A laptop gets its reserved IP address on Wi-Fi, but a random address from the pool when it sits on its desk dock with a network cable. Nothing else changed. Why?

A DHCP reservation matches a MAC address (lesson 0014). Docked, the laptop uses the dock's network adapter, which has its own MAC, so the server sees a different client and hands out a normal pool address. Reserve the dock adapter's MAC too, or use the one that matters.

From memory: two differences between an active and a passive stylus, and two between a docking station and a port replicator. (then click to check)

Active stylus: has electronics/battery; pressure sensitivity and palm rejection via the digitizer; needs device support. Passive: none of that, works like a finger anywhere. Docking station: adds ports beyond the laptop's own and charges it, often a proprietary connector. Port replicator: copies existing ports, traditionally no charging, smaller.

Review: earlier lessons

Spaced retrieval: pull these from memory first, then check.

You're replacing a laptop's HDD with an SSD and keeping the user's files and apps. Which two things do you do before the swap? (lesson 0023, 3 days ago)

Clone the old drive to the new one (for example with a USB-to-SATA adapter), and suspend BitLocker first so the hardware change doesn't trigger a recovery-key prompt. Check the form factor too: 2.5-inch or M.2.

Put these tools in the order a cable's life uses them: cable tester, crimper, tap, toner probe, cable stripper. (lesson 0019, 7 days ago)

Build: stripper, crimper (punch-down for the wall end) → find: toner probe → test: cable tester → watch: tap.

A domain has two MX records, preference 10 and 20. Which server gets mail first, and what record type would you use to make www an alias of another name? (lesson 0012, 14 days ago)

The lowest number wins: preference 10 first, 20 is the backup. An alias is a CNAME record.
