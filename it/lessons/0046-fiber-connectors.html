Twist, push, click: fiber connectors

About 10 minutes. Yesterday's connectors were copper; today's end a glass fiber (lesson 0039). The exam names three: ST, SC and LC.[1] The win: tell them apart by how they lock and how big they are, and know which one plugs into a switch's fiber port.

Why this matters at work

When a fiber link between two switches or to the provider's box needs a new patch cable, you have to order the right ends. A cable with LC on one end and SC on the other is common and cheap, but only if you checked both ports first. The wrong ends mean the link waits days for a reorder.

1. The three connectors

ConnectorShape and lockWhere you'll see it
ST (straight tip)Round metal barrel; bayonet lock: push in and twist a quarter turn, untwist to remove. 2.5 mm ferrule.[2][3]Older multimode networks and patch panels; legacy gear.
SC (subscriber / square connector)Square plastic body; push-pull: snaps in straight, pulls straight out. 2.5 mm ferrule.[2][3]Provider fiber and the home or office ONT (lesson 0021), older switch ports.
LC (local / Lucent connector)Small, about half the size of SC; latch clip on top like an RJ45. 1.25 mm ferrule. Usually two side by side (duplex).[2][3]Today's switches, servers and SFP transceivers; dense patch panels.

The ferrule is the thin ceramic tube in the tip that holds the fiber dead center, so two cores line up face to face. LC's smaller ferrule is why it fits twice as many ports in the same space.[3]

2. Two fibers, one conversation

Light goes one way down a strand, so a normal link uses two fibers: one transmit, one receive.[2] That's why LC usually comes as a clipped-together pair. If a new link stays dark, one classic cause is the pair landing swapped (transmit into transmit). Many duplex connectors let you flip the pair without re-terminating.

3. Colors on the connector body

  • Blue body: single-mode, UPC polish (flat-domed tip).
  • Green body: single-mode, APC polish (tip angled at 8°), common on provider fiber and ONTs.
  • Beige body: often multimode.

Never mate a green APC plug with a blue UPC port: the angled and flat faces don't meet, the link loses light, and the tips can be damaged. Match the color of the plug to the port.[4] (The cable jacket colors from lesson 0039 are a different code: yellow single-mode, orange or aqua multimode.)

Memory hook

ST = Stick and Twist (bayonet). SC = Stick and Click (square, push-pull). LC = Little Connector (half size, latch, in pairs).

Try it (2 minutes)

  1. If you have fiber internet, look at where the provider's fiber enters the ONT (don't unplug it). Square body? That's SC. Green? APC polish.
  2. At work, look at a switch with fiber uplinks (or a photo of one). The small removable modules are SFP transceivers, and the paired plugs in them are duplex LC.
  3. Safety rule from lesson 0039: never look into a fiber end or port, and put the dust caps back on.
A connector is square, snaps in without twisting, and is about twice the size of the plugs next to it on the switch. Which one is it, and which ones are the small plugs? (then click to check)

The square push-pull one is SC. The smaller latch-clip plugs are LC, half the size thanks to a 1.25 mm ferrule instead of 2.5 mm.

Practice

Answer from memory before scrolling back up.

In an old wiring closet you find a round metal fiber connector that only comes out after a quarter turn. What is it?

ST (straight tip) uses a bayonet coupling: push and twist to lock, untwist to release. SC and LC go straight in and out; the F connector screws on but carries copper coax (lesson 0045).

A switch has a row of small removable fiber modules, each with two tiny plugs clipped together. Which connector is in them?

The modules are SFP transceivers, and those almost always take duplex LC: small (1.25 mm ferrule), latch clip, one fiber to transmit and one to receive.

A new fiber link between two switches stays dark. Both ends have LC plugs seated firmly and the cable tests good. What's a likely cause?

A duplex link needs one end's transmit to reach the other end's receive. If the pair is crossed the wrong way, no light arrives where it's expected. Flipping the pair at one end is the usual fix. LC locks with a latch, not a twist.

A technician has a patch cable with green SC plugs. The ONT's port is green too. What does the green tell them?

Green marks APC (angled, 8°) polish, common on provider fiber. Blue is UPC. Green plug into green port is correct; mixing APC and UPC loses light and can damage the tips.

From memory: for ST, SC and LC, give the lock type and one place you'd find each. (then click to check)

ST bayonet twist, older multimode panels · SC square push-pull, provider fiber and ONTs · LC small latch, in duplex pairs, SFP modules in switches.

Review: earlier lessons

Spaced retrieval: pull these from memory first, then check.

A DSL modem and a cable modem sit side by side. Which connector brings the provider's line into each? (lesson 0045, yesterday)

DSL: an RJ11 phone cord. Cable: a threaded F connector on coax (usually RG-6).

In SaaS like Microsoft 365, what does the provider manage, and what stays your job no matter the model? (lesson 0032, 14 days ago)

The provider runs almost everything: hardware, OS, the app itself. In every model (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) the customer keeps its data, its endpoints, its accounts and access management.

Split tunnel vs. full tunnel: where does a user's ordinary web traffic go in each? (lesson 0016, 30 days ago)

Full tunnel: everything, web included, goes through the VPN to the company first. Split tunnel: only company traffic uses the VPN; ordinary web traffic goes straight out the local internet connection.
